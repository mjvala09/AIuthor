# Understanding Debt and Credit Scores

## Good Debt vs. Bad Debt
Not all debt is created equal. Understanding the difference between good debt and bad debt is critical for financial health.

### 1. Good Debt
Good debt is debt that has the potential to increase your net worth or generate future income. It typically carries low-interest rates.
- **Mortgages**: Allows you to buy a home, which historically appreciates in value. Mortgages also offer potential tax benefits and leverage.
- **Student Loans**: An investment in your earning potential. Statistically, college graduates earn significantly more over their lifetime than high school graduates.

### 2. Bad Debt
Bad debt is debt used to purchase depreciating assets or consumable goods. It carries high interest rates and drains your monthly cash flow.
- **Credit Card Debt**: Typically carries interest rates between 15% and 30%. Carrying a balance on a credit card is the most common financial trap.
- **Payday Loans**: Short-term loans with predatory interest rates that can exceed 400% APR.
- **Car Loans for Expensive Vehicles**: A car is a rapidly depreciating asset. Financing a car with a high monthly payment limits your ability to save and invest.

## Debt Payoff Strategies
If you have multiple debts, there are two primary methods to tackle them:

### 1. The Debt Snowball Method
Developed by Dave Ramsey, this method focuses on psychological wins:
1. List all your debts from **smallest balance to largest balance**, regardless of the interest rates.
2. Make minimum payments on all debts except the smallest.
3. Throw all extra cash at the smallest debt until it is paid off.
4. Take the entire amount you were paying on the smallest debt and roll it into the next smallest debt.
- **Benefit**: Quick wins keep you motivated.

### 2. The Debt Avalanche Method
This method focuses on mathematical efficiency:
1. List all your debts from **highest interest rate to lowest interest rate**.
2. Make minimum payments on all debts except the one with the highest interest rate.
3. Throw all extra cash at the highest interest rate debt.
4. Once paid off, roll the payments into the next highest interest rate debt.
- **Benefit**: Saves the most money in interest and pays off debt faster.

## Credit Scores and Credit Reports
A credit score is a numerical representation of a borrower's creditworthiness, ranging from 300 to 850 in the FICO model. A high credit score enables you to qualify for lower interest rates on loans, saving tens of thousands of dollars.

### FICO Credit Score Factors:
- **35% Payment History**: Whether you pay your bills on time. A single 30-day late payment can drop your score significantly.
- **30% Amounts Owed (Credit Utilization)**: The percentage of your available credit limit that you are currently using. It is recommended to keep this **under 30%**, and ideally under 10%. If your limit is $10,000, your balance should not exceed $3,000.
- **15% Length of Credit History**: The average age of all your credit accounts. Older is better.
- **10% New Credit**: How many credit inquiries you have made recently. Too many hard inquiries in a short period suggests financial stress.
- **10% Credit Mix**: The variety of credit accounts you hold (e.g., credit cards, auto loans, mortgages).
